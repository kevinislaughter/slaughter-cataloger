*A Weekly Record of Town and Country*

VOL. IV. — NO. 1. — WHOLE NO. 157. — ANYTOWN, SATURDAY, MAY 1, 1875. — PRICE TEN CENTS.

### TO OUR READERS

With this number the *Gazette* enters upon its fourth volume, and upon a new plan: it will appear once a fortnight instead of once a week, in eight pages instead of four. Our motto, 𝔉𝔦𝔡𝔢𝔩𝔦𝔱𝔞𝔰, stands where it stood.

We thank the many readers who wrote to us during the winter. Their letters are printed, as space allows, on page 4.

*(continued on page 2)*

### A CORRECTION

In our last number the meeting of the Improvement Society was given as Tuesday. It is to be held on Thursday, at the Town Hall, at eight o’clock.

---

### AROUND THE COUNTY

Notes from our correspondents in the townships. Contributions should reach the office by the Monday before publication.

#### Eastfield

The Reading Circle met on the 20th at the schoolhouse. Officers were chosen for the year, and the library committee reported forty-two volumes on the shelves. — JANE DOE.

*(Around the County continued on page 2.)*

---

“Let every man be swift to hear.”

— *From an old almanac.*

## PDF page 2 — printed page 2

THE EXAMPLE GAZETTE. 2

### TO OUR READERS (continued from page 1)

The new plan will allow room for longer reports from the townships, and for the market prices, which many have asked for. The subscription remains two dollars a year. — THE EDITOR.

### AROUND THE COUNTY (continued)

#### Riverton

The ferry resumed its crossings on the 12th, the river having fallen. A new landing is talked of. — R. R.

#### Lakeside

The ice went out of the lake on April 9, nine days later than last year. — A CORRESPONDENT.

---

### THE NEW BRIDGE

The county commissioners met on Wednesday to open the bids for the iron bridge over the Mill River. Four bids were received, the lowest from a firm in Springfield.

#### A Word for the Old Road

Sir, — I have travelled the old road to Riverton for thirty years and see no need of a new one. Mend the old one, and spare us the \*improvements\*.

[Communicated.]

— A SUBSCRIBER.

Sir, — When are we to have lamps on Elm Street? The darkness there is a public danger. — J.Q.P.
